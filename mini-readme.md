# VoidCompass // UPDATE LOG

## v5.7.6 // Changing of the Watch
**Release Date:** 7 NOV 3312 *(Galactic Standard Time)*

* **Meet Lyra, the fifth companion.** She was a ship's computer. Her first commander was a great pilot who flew too far and ran out of fuel, and when a passing cargo ship turned up with an empty memory chip in its hold, Lyra jumped aboard. Now she's yours: bright, exact and very fond of a full tank. She has her own voice (a robotic Piper voice, downloaded like the others), her own story, *The Empty Chip*, in eight chapters with echoes that stir on real journal events and a request after Chapter III (take her to a star nobody has discovered), her own name arc, postcard notes, Captain's Log paragraphs and share code, and her own words for everything the other officers talk about, in two styles, Bright and System. Say "hello Lyra" in TALK, or put her name before a command. Four achievements of her own: Power On, Full Tanks, Every Gauge Read and Uncharted.
* **Lyra's face moves.** Her jaw follows her voice, and her face wears the mood of what she's saying: a wider smile and squinted eyes when she's pleased, wide eyes and swept-back fins when she's wary, a frown and drooping fins when she's downcast, a tilted head when she's curious. Low fuel, overheating or a threat worries her whatever she's saying, and her face settles back to rest when the thought has gone. Reduced motion changes it at once instead of easing.
* **The officers show how they feel.** Aster, Axiom and Meridian now wear the mood of what they're saying too, each in its own way. Meridian smiles, frowns, tilts its head when curious and tightens its rings when wary. Axiom's particles ripple warm when it's pleased, pull tight and flicker when it's wary, sink when it's downcast and drift to one side when it's curious, and its crystal turns to look. Aster's halo pulses twice to acknowledge good news, its core holds still when it's wary, a scan crosses the prism when it's curious, and it dims when it's downcast.
* **A moment of their own.** While the drive charges on a plotted route, a needle on Meridian's ring swings to the next star. Axiom's crystal lights a facet for each body you scan in the system, and glows whole when every body is found. When docking is granted, a light runs round Aster's halo and your pad number shows in its core until you've docked.
* **Three officers, three instruments.** Each officer now has its own bezel and instruments, all the same size as before. Aster has a command bezel with rank chevrons; it rings the ship's watch bells every half hour (one bell at half past, up to eight at the end of each four-hour watch), and with the watch rota on, a watch bill round its core lights the duty you're on and runs a light round at a handover. Axiom has a measuring scale; a tally round it marks each body it has counted in the system, and its particle field carries a map of the system, a dot for each scanned body by its distance and kind. Meridian has a brass compass rose; its needle points home when you're at rest (or to the core when home is close), a sounding gauge down one side shows how far from home you are, and a wake ripples across the rings each time you jump.
* **The crew knows her.** Each of the others has a new crew passage about her coming aboard, and she has her own account of meeting each of them. She hands over and takes the watch by name, joins the debriefs and the Night Watch scenes, and is answered by name when you ask a crewmate in TALK. Full Crew now means flying with all five, and The Watch Rota means hearing all 35 passages.
* **A watch rota (optional).** Turn on Watch rota on the companion page (under Its thoughts) and the crew changes the watch with what you're doing, within about a minute of it starting. You choose who takes each duty: docked (Aster by default), scanning in the FSS or DSS (Axiom), on a planet's surface on foot or in an SRV (Axiom), on a plotted route (Meridian) deep space, from 1,000 light years out by default, or 250 to 10,000 as you choose (the Watcher), and ordinary flight with none of those (Lyra); or "Whoever's on watch" to leave a duty alone. The rota waits at least three minutes between changes, choosing a companion yourself holds your choice for five minutes, and every change is a proper handover, by name. Off by default, saved per profile.
* **The crew sees you off.** When an expedition sets out, the companion on watch marks the start and each crewmate you've flown with adds a line of their own, one after another, off watch: Meridian on the route and fuel, Axiom on what's worth scanning, Aster on the paperwork, the Watcher in its own way.
* **Captain's Log postscripts.** When more than one companion stood a watch in a session, the flight log is written by the one on watch and signed off with a short postscript from each of the others.
* **A story for the whole crew.** Once all five companions have told how they came aboard, they begin a story together: a scene now and then in a quiet moment, at least two hours apart, a line from each of them in turn and in their own voices. It's shared, so whoever is on watch carries on where the last one stopped, and the STORY tab keeps every scene you've heard.
* **The crew in Void Wrapped.** A new card for your year (or your expedition): how many hours each companion stood watch and how many watches, the stories each one told, a line each of them actually said to you, and who stood the most of it.
* **Rhino survey.** Mark a survey's centre where you stand (Ctrl+Alt+7), drive out and mark its edge (Ctrl+Alt+8). The keys can be changed in Settings › Hotkeys, and the Mining page has buttons for both. Void Compass then shades everything your SRV's scanner has reached inside the circle (2 km round you), gives the coverage as a percentage, and lays out sweep stops whose scanner circles cover the whole area, pointing you to the nearest one not yet swept. Coverage is kept per planet, so it survives a restart. Your companion says when the survey is set, half covered and done.
* **The best patch.** The Rhino map now rings the richest patch so far: refining pulses gathered within 150 m of each other, counted. Elite gives no deposit positions, so it's only ever where you stood for each pulse. Pulses are now kept per planet too. Your companion says when a best patch first shows and when another takes the lead.
* **A rig pattern.** After your first rig (or round the best patch, before any rig is down), the Rhino map shows a hex pattern at the rig spacing (78 m) for the rigs still to place, and RIG HERE points to the nearest spot in it. A spot far from your other rigs is flagged.
* **On every Rhino screen.** The survey's coverage, sweep stops and next stop, the best patch and the rig pattern are on the Rhino map; the Rig Tracker shows the survey %, the next stop, the best patch and the next rig spot with distance and direction; and the Mining page has a RHINO SURVEY panel with all of it.
* **TALK knows where things are.** Ask "where's the nearest material trader?" (or raw, manufactured or encoded trader, Interstellar Factors, technology broker, shipyard, outfitting, black market, Universal Cartographics, Vista Genomics, refuel, repair, rearm, Search and Rescue, Pioneer Supplies, Apex, Frontline Solutions, bartender, refinery contact, redemption office, or just a station) and your companion searches Spansh from where you are: the three nearest, how far, how far in from the star and whether they have large pads. Fleet carriers are left out unless you ask for one. It uses the uplink, so trading searches must be on in Settings, under Integrations.
* **"How far is Colonia?"** Ask how far it is to any system. Your companion answers from your own travel history first, then from well-known places (Sol, Colonia, Beagle Point, Sagittarius A*) and your home, and only goes to Spansh, with trading searches on, for a system you've never been to. The answer includes about how many jumps it is at your jump range. Route questions ("how far to the next system?") still get the route.
* **Materials by grade.** "How much arsenic do I have?" now works without the word "materials", and says the grade and the limit ("Arsenic: 42 of 250 (raw, grade 2)"). "Materials by grade" and "grade 4 raw materials" give totals by grade, how many are full, and the most plentiful.
* **Community goals.** Ask "how are my community goals doing?" (or "any CGs?"): each goal you've joined, where it is, your contribution, your percentile band, the tier reached and the time left, kept from Elite's own CommunityGoal reports.
* **Officers who sum you up.** Aster, Axiom, Meridian and Lyra have more to say about your habits (flying, scanning, cargo, combat, trade) and new lines about your style that read properly ("By my figures you are mostly an explorer, at 62 percent"); the old ones said things like "leans towards an explorer".
* **Docking, answered every time.** After you asked for docking (most often by voice or TALK, whose reply just spoke), your companion stayed silent when the station granted it: the grant waited behind the reply, could go unsaid on a quiet setting or on a busy docking day, and wasn't said twice within twenty minutes. Now the station's answer to your request is always said, aloud and in fresh words: granted (with your pad), denied (and why), cancelled, or timed out, the last two being new. A request made from the panel is announced too ("Requesting docking at Jameson Memorial"); one you asked for by voice isn't said twice.
* **Companions who notice more of your game.** We checked the journal schema event by event: your companions now speak to 55 more moments. Danger and the law: a cockpit breach, a Thargoid shutdown, self destruct, crimes you commit (in plain words, with the fine or bounty) and ones done to you, paying fines and bounties, an impounded ship released, reboot repairs, and pulling someone out of supercruise. Missions: accepted, failed, abandoned, redirected (go back for the pay), Search and Rescue handed in, and community goals joined, paid out and left. Ships: buying, selling and naming one, outfitting, transfers, engineers inviting you, unlocking and ranking up, new materials and material trades. Your carrier (bought, renamed, decommissioned or kept, tritium, trade orders), colonies (claimed, released, the beacon, deliveries) and Powerplay (joining, leaving, defecting, merits, salary). And the people and kit around you: suits, weapons and upgrades, dropship deployments, multicrew, hired pilots, and wing invites. All five companions, in their own words.
* **Engineering, noticed.** Your companions now speak when an engineer finishes a grade (not on every roll), make more of a finished grade 5 and of an experimental effect, and notice engineer unlock deliveries and technology broker unlocks. They keep count of your materials from the game's own list, so they tell you once when one reaches its storage limit, and pick out a grade 5 material when you collect one. Blueprints are said the way you'd say them ("Increased Range on the frame shift drive"). In TALK, ask "which engineers have I unlocked?", "what rank am I with Farseer?" or "which materials are full?".
* **FOLLOW ME after a restart.** Before the journal has said where you are, the Galactic Atlas no longer treats 0, 0, 0 as your position, which could send FOLLOW ME to Sol until your next jump. FOLLOW ME itself was already kept between sessions.
* **Hotkeys in their groups.** Settings › Hotkeys no longer puts every other shortcut under OVERLAY CONTROLS. Two new groups, NAVIGATION (Navigation HUD pages, Galaxy Minimap view) and RHINO, RIGS AND SURVEY (the rig keys, Rhino Map range and the survey keys), join EXPEDITIONS AND COLONIES (mark this place, Construction Needs fold and refresh); OVERLAY CONTROLS keeps just show/hide all and the Layout Studio.
* **Companion settings set once.** The companion's Overlay Studio settings (size, focus, idle animation and liveliness) now apply to every companion, so you set them once instead of once each, and switching companions keeps them. Colour stays each companion's own.
