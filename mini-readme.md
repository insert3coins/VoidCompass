# VoidCompass // UPDATE LOG

## v5.7.4 // Aster, Officer on Watch
**Release Date:** TBA 3312 *(Galactic Standard Time)*

* **Aster, officer on watch.** Choose Aster or The Watcher on the Watcher page. Aster has an original six-chapter story, its own greetings and command replies, a separate Piper voice and a live Three.js instrument with Studio size, accent and motion controls. Each companion keeps its own memory for every commander.
* **A companion of its own.** Aster has 48 original story passages across six chapters, 50 greetings, ten capability rundowns and its own event and command replies. Choose the officer’s commentary, enable its Piper voice on the Watcher page, and adjust its instrument size, accent and motion in Overlay Studio. Shared commands, aliases and sequences continue to work with either companion.
* **Safe switching.** Changing companion stops queued speech, rejects late recognition and lookup replies, and keeps each companion’s story clock, voice preferences and saved memory separate for every commander.
* **Companion controls that follow your selection.** The deck and Studio use the selected companion’s name. A single display inspector controls size, accent, animation intensity and idle motion, with immediate resizing. Aster uses the newer British-English Piper pair under `models/jarvis/onnx/`, with verified download hashes and its own download labels.
* **Aster’s luminous halo.** Flowing Three.js light trails surround a hexagonal command core with rotating arcs and scanning traces, giving Aster a distinct transparent display and matching officer’s panel. It follows flight, journal and voice activity. Both companions expose the same Studio size, colour, focus movement, idle animation, liveliness and frame-rate controls, with separate colour and motion preferences.
* **A steadier watch.** Display size carries across companion switches and matches visually. Aster has over 1,400 event wordings and varied replies for every command outcome, including restrained commentary. Greeting, capability and command rotations survive restarts; idle remarks follow the Watcher’s pacing and keep quiet when fresh wording runs out. Spoken captions wait for their queued line and follow playback through long replies.
* **One way of speaking on screen.** Aster and Watcher share the same timed text in their floating overlay and NOW panel: character-by-character typing with punctuation pauses, following the active voice. Queued replies wait their turn, and the panel keeps its controls steady while the line appears.
* **Quieter hatchling commentary.** Aster’s pet remarks follow a shared cooldown and idle pacing, including notices from the hatchling timer. Replayed moments stay silent, and toy, trick and growth reports describe the actual event in Aster’s own wording.
* **A name in the crew register.** Aster learns how you wish to be addressed through a short, original introduction. Choose your journal name, Commander or a nickname using the panel, answer keys, TALK or voice, with saved pronunciation and passages you can reread under YOUR NAME. Your approved name becomes its regular form of address throughout replies and reports.
* **Company in port.** Aster has varied dockside, carrier and concourse conversation, including restrained replies. Port remarks follow your chosen frequency, remain available on Rare, and recognise a docked startup without requiring another arrival.
* **A watch with a memory.** Aster can discuss learned flying/trading/survey habits, recall old postcards and service moments, and reflect on story chapters you have already heard. These use its own officer lore, with remembered cooldowns, contextual call replies and a gentle reminder during long sessions.
* **Postcards from your companion.** Automatic postcard notes follow the selected companion. Aster writes and signs its own officer's notes, with plain or dry wording; previews, saved pictures, the editor and text captions retain the correct author.
* **A clear end-of-watch report.** Aster's shutdown summary explicitly describes the completed session, with the jump count separated from its farewell and the totals included in every report variant. Larger active/quiet farewell banks have saved full rotations, keeping consecutive shutdowns different across restarts.
* **A welcome on login.** Aster acknowledges each new game login, including quick relogs, with a saved greeting rotation and your chosen address. Duplicate login events and an immediate early-wake welcome are handled once.
