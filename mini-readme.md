# VoidCompass // UPDATE LOG

## v5.8.3 // Paper Trail
**Release Date:** TBA

* **Your stream's chat talks to your companion.** Switch on Settings › Integrations › Stream chat, type your Twitch channel, and your viewers have a crew member to talk to. It only reads chat (no Twitch account, nothing ever posted), and it reconnects by itself.
  * **They ask, it answers:** !where, !ship, !route, !session (tonight's time on air, jumps, light years, scans, first discoveries, biology, tonnes and credits: it knows when you're live, asking Twitch once a minute, so the clock and the figures start when you go live, stop when you go off air, and Settings and the overlay say ON AIR or OFF AIR), !rigs, !crew, !rank, !top and !ask a question about the ship, the system's bodies and life, planets, ranks or Galnet. The companion on watch answers in its own voice, naming who asked, and shows it on the new Stream Chat overlay with the viewer's name in their own chat colour. It only speaks to answer a command: ordinary chat and commands it doesn't know get nothing, and everything else it has to say (welcomes, thanks, the naming votes, promotions) is shown, not said. Moderators have !quiet, !unquiet and !credits.
  * **It knows your crew:** newcomers are welcomed, regulars welcomed back by name ("fourth stream running"), and raids, subs, gifts and bits thanked on the overlay, each companion in its own way.
  * **Crew ranks:** naming worlds earns points (the winning name, and a vote for it) and a crew rank, from Recruit up to Legend of the Black; !rank and !top show them.
  * **Chat names your discoveries:** be first to an Earth-like, water or ammonia world (or a terraformable one) and chat suggests names with !name, then votes with !vote. The winning name is shown on the overlay and kept with the world.
  * **Roll the credits:** at the end of a stream, ROLL THE CREDITS (in Settings, a hotkey, a moderator's !credits or the Local API) scrolls them up the overlay like a film's: the flight's figures, the top crew, worlds named, raids and supporters, and everyone who chatted, then your companion signs off.
  * **A chat box in the Neon looks:** the Stream Chat overlay is a chat box like Neon's: Neon rail, Bubbles, Boxed panel, Clean, or Cards in sixteen styles (Synthwave, Cybernetik, Glitch, Iridescent, Champion, Nova Pixel and more), set in Overlay Studio with the accent (or your companion's own colour) and the box's height. Viewers' lines come in at the bottom with their own colours, role badges, and Twitch and BetterTTV emotes (global, channel-owned and shared channel emotes), "First chat" and "Welcome back" chips, and raids, subs and bits as their own rows; your companion's answers and lines sit among them in its colour, tagged with who asked. The game and the banners ride on top as neon cards, and the credits roll in neon. Commands, other bots and anything with a blocked word stay out of the box, and it follows your moderators: a deleted message comes off it, a viewer timed out or banned loses their lines (and the answers to them, and their guesses and suggested names in a game), and /clear empties it; Viewers' chat switches it to answers only, and the Void Compass HUD look is still there.
  * **Emotes catch up with chat:** BetterTTV emotes load in the background. Messages already in the box update when their emotes arrive, without replaying their entrance animation.
  * **Safe on air:** viewers' words are never said aloud, only their names, and those only if they pass the filter (add your own words in Settings); a world name that doesn't pass isn't shown. Nothing chat says can press a key or change the ship. Replies wait while you're in danger, interdicted or docking, a hotkey mutes them, moderators have !quiet, and each viewer has a cooldown. Against stream sniping, !where can give the exact place, only the region, or where you were 10 or 30 minutes ago.
  * TRY !WHERE, !SESSION and !ASK in Settings show and say a reply as if from chat. The Local API has it too: GET /api/v1/stream, POST /api/v1/stream/credits and /stream/mute, and a `stream` event for each answer.
* **Overlays follow the game, from startup.** Hide when Elite is not running checks for the game before opening overlays, then every two seconds through exits, crashes and relaunches. With it on, every overlay, including the music player, hides while Elite is closed. Gameplay overlays return when you load into the game; the music player can stay at the main menu while Elite is running. Reading an old journal cannot bring overlays back onto the desktop.
* **Game-closed hiding is on by default.** New installs, new commander profiles and profiles without a saved choice start with Hide when Elite is not running switched on. You can switch it off in Overlay Studio, and that choice is saved per commander and remembered across restarts.
* **Visibility controls where you can see them.** A VISIBILITY - ALL OVERLAYS card now sits at the top of Overlay Studio, above the layout editor, with the game-closed, map, menu and Layout mode switches together. A live status explains when overlays are waiting for Elite, waiting for you to load in, or being previewed in Layout mode. Each overlay's Hide on maps and Hide in menus switches can always be changed; a note explains when the matching All overlays switch needs turning on. Map hiding covers the Galaxy Map, System Map and Orrery. Layout mode still lets you place overlays with the game closed, with a reminder that it temporarily overrides hiding.
* **The opacity slider matches the overlays.** Missing or invalid saved opacity values now show the same default in Overlay Studio as the windows actually use, instead of the slider showing 40% while the overlays are fully opaque.
* **Overlay groups by name.** Ask your companion, typed or by voice: "show the Rhino group", "hide the exploring group", "switch to the combat group", "toggle the mining group".
* **Groups on each overlay.** Overlay Studio's inspector shows which groups the overlay you've picked is in, with a switch for each to put it in or take it out.
* **Share your themes.** COPY SHARE CODE in the Theme Workshop puts your theme on the clipboard as a short code (VCTHEME-…), name, colours and skin, ready to post. PASTE A SHARED THEME (beside the workshop button) loads someone else's code for you to try on the whole deck before you save it.
* **The Rhino page, redone.** It had grown one panel at a time; now it's laid out round how you use it, in four tabs that carry their counts. A bar along the top says where you are in big type (the body, then its system), with the signal, what you're in, each hold as a gauge and the run going.
  * **MAP:** the site you're at comes first (its name, saved or not, and a link to it in SITES), then the keys grouped the way you work a signal (SURVEY, DEPOSIT, PLAN, with CLEAR SURVEY and FORGET RIGS kept apart at the bottom), the deposit you picked and the deposits here. Under the map, a card for each of the six rigs: what it's working and its tonnes, which way and how far, and its clock in big figures (time to READY, READY in green, COLLECTING in yellow), with its key to put it down or pick it up. Out of the Rhino, the page says what it's waiting for and takes you to SITES, DEPOSITS or WHERE TO LAND.
  * **SITES:** every saved site compared on one scale (its usual tonnes an hour as a bar, its best run marked on it, its last twelve runs as dots, hover one for the run; the best place to go back to at the top), then a list of your sites to pick from (the one you're at first) and the one picked in full: where it is down to its centre, its sweep, deposits, runs and rate, what it's worth, its runs with a chart and the best one in green, what each commodity took and is worth at your sale price (else the usual one), and the deposits saved with it, each with EDIT and TARGET. TARGET, RESUME, COPY SYSTEM, RENAME, REPORT and DELETE sit under its name.
  * **DEPOSITS:** every deposit you've marked, on every planet, with worked-out ones due a look first (the tab says how many are due). EDIT on a deposit opens its editor right under that row, and DELETE is on the row itself, asking twice.
* **Site reports.** REPORT on a saved site writes it up: where it is (system, body, signal, centre and its survey), its deposits, its runs with tonnes an hour, and what each commodity took and is worth. COPY FOR DISCORD puts it on the clipboard as plain text with bold headings that fits one Discord message; SAVE AS A FILE puts it in a folder you choose. Other apps can have it too: GET /api/v1/rhino/sites/{id}/report.
* **The Rig Tracker, made to be read.** A card for each rig out, two to a line, instead of six small dials: its number in a ring that fills to READY, what it's working and its tonnes, which way and how far it is, and a big clock: how long to READY, READY in green, or COLLECTING with the tonnes coming in as you collect; once it's filling again, what that collection gave ("last 11 t") sits beside the clock, and the Rhino page says the same. Free rig numbers go on one line, and nothing on the overlay is smaller than it should be, so the whole thing reads at a glance (the overlay's text size still makes it bigger). NEAREST and STATUS are gone, and their switches in Overlay Studio: the rigs and the mineral deposit say what matters, the rig radar's word (RIG OK HERE, TOO CLOSE…) has a line of its own under the rigs, and the Rhino Map and the Rhino page keep the values and patches. The overlay is shorter than it was.
* **Rig timers that follow the rig.** A rig keeps working once you've collected from it, and fills again. Its clock counts from placement, then from the last tonne collected at that rig, on the Rig Tracker and the Rhino page. Leaving HERE (within 25 m, now fixed) or half a minute without another tonne ends the collection without adding that waiting time to the countdown. The journal's own timestamps keep processing delays out of the learned time. Tonnes the refinery finishes after you've left still count for that rig and don't restart its clock; returning without collecting doesn't change it either. Each rig keeps its clock through an app restart. Collection intervals refine the estimated ready time, by deposit density when enough have been measured; early partial collections aren't used. READY is an estimate: the game doesn't report the exact moment a rig becomes full.
* **READY when the clock says so.** Your companion tells you a rig is ready when its time is up, wherever you are, and again each time it fills (it used to wait until you were beside it).
* **Deposits stay put across a wide survey.** The deposits you marked at a mining location dropped off the Rhino page, the Rhino Map and the Rig Tracker once you were 8 km from them, so on the far side of a big survey (one marked out to 6 km or more) the deposits on its other edge vanished as you drove round it. Everything inside the survey you're working now counts as here, wherever you are in it, as do the deposits saved with the site you're at. A survey marked out wider than 8 km is no longer put away as "a new mining location" when you drive out to its edge.
* **Pads at surface stations, learnt as you land.** Nobody has mapped which numbered pad is where at planetary ports, surface outposts and Odyssey settlements, so the app learns them: when docking is granted it notes your pad, and when you land on it, it remembers exactly where that pad is. The next time you're given that pad there, your companion says how far and which way ("Pad 4: 380 m ahead-left"), it's on the Dashboard's pad card, and the planet compass takes you to it, coming off as you dock. A pad it hasn't learnt yet is said so, and remembered as you land. Each landing refines the spot. It learns each station design too (its type and pads by size, and a settlement's economy): stations of one design share a layout, each turned its own way, so after one landing at another of that design, from where you landed and which way you faced, it works out where all that station's other pads are ("worked out from this station's design"). Every later landing checks it, and a design that keeps missing isn't used. Settings › Flight & exploration › Surface pads shows what's been learnt, and EXPORT PAD LAYOUTS saves it as a plain JSON file to share; IMPORT PAD LAYOUTS pools other commanders' files with yours (your own landings are kept), and other apps can read the same at GET /api/v1/pads. (Orbital stations and carriers keep their pad diagrams.)
* **Coming in to a settlement.** As you approach a settlement or surface port, your companion says how far it is and which way, and which of its pads are known already, before you've asked for docking: "Vries Holdings, 12.4 km ahead-left. I know 3 of 16 pads here: you've landed on pad 14 and 2 more are worked out from its design." Somewhere with landing pads you've not landed at yet is said too, so you know its pads will be learnt. Once each half hour a place at most, and it follows Settings' pad callout switch. (The game doesn't say which design a settlement is until docking, so its pads are known from your own landings there.)
* **Your route keeps up after a restart.** If Elite crashed and you started VoidCompass again (or started it after you'd already jumped), the jumps it read back from the journal didn't move your route on, so it stayed at an old stop. Once it has read back the journal, your route is put at the system you're in, quietly, and carries on from there.
* **Copy the next destination from its name.** On the Dashboard's route card, click the next system's name and it's on the clipboard, ready to paste into the galaxy map (COPY NEXT DESTINATION under it still does the same).
* **The Journal Log, a page of its own.** Under RECORDS in the rail, and it can pop out into its own window like any page: every journal event as one readable line, searchable, by kind and how far back, with the raw event a click away. Explore & Survey's ARCHIVE has a button straight to it.

## v5.8.2 // Ready When You Are
**Release Date:** 15 NOV 3312 *(Galactic Standard Time)*

* **The Theme Workshop, rebuilt.** Settings › Appearance now groups the themes into SKINS (their own fonts and shapes), PALETTES and YOURS, and the workshop opens from a button under them, or EDIT on one of yours, instead of a drop-down at the bottom. The whole deck shows your theme as you change it, until you save it or close the workshop. Start from any theme (or click one while the workshop's open), give yours any skin's fonts and shapes, set each colour by name or hex, pick a background, text and accent and BLEND THE REST, and see how readable the text is as you go. Rename or delete one of yours from the workshop too.
* **Rig ready times that learn.** The READY ring on each rig dial never appeared: a rig was timed from its key down to its key up, and a rig often stays down all run, so every time was thrown out as too long. A rig is now timed to the first tonne refined round it (you start collecting once it's ready), as it happens, and two timed rigs are enough to start.
* **Saved sites without the clutter.** Driving about a planet with deposits on it could save an empty site wherever you were, and the same deposits came back as a second site when their middle moved. A site is now only kept by itself with a survey or deposits in it, the same deposits are always the same site, and a visit without a survey no longer drops the one a site was saved with. Empty sites you already have are cleared out, and doubles are folded together, keeping the first one's name.
* **Each site's runs, kept.** Runs often weren't kept, as the game can drop your position for a moment while the Rhino docks. The Rhino's last position is used then, and a run goes to the nearest saved site within the mining location rather than only one within 2 km of its centre.
* **One welcome a session.** Your companion said welcome back again every time you went to the main menu and back, and a companion coming on watch greeted you once more. It's now once each time the app starts.
* **The planet waypoint while you fly in.** With Navigation HUD pages on, the HUD shows the Planet Waypoint for you, but only brought its PLANET page up once you'd landed, so flying in to a target nothing showed it. A waypoint on the body below now brings the page up from the ship too.
* **Overlays hide when you leave the game.** Overlay Studio's Hide with the game closed is now on for everyone (switch it off there if you'd rather keep them), and the main menu counts as well as a shutdown: exit to the menu or the desktop and every overlay but the music player goes, and they're back as you load into the game. A crash is still noticed within 20 seconds.
* **The region in the status bar.** The status bar at the bottom of the deck said REGION UNKNOWN until your first jump of the session. It now reads your region from where you are, as the top bar does.

## v5.8.1 // Kings of the Crust
**Release Date:** 15 NOV 3312 *(Galactic Standard Time)*

* **Rhino Mining, a page of its own.** Everything for the Rhino round one big map: the keys as buttons (survey centre and edge, mark a deposit, trace, planning circle, rigs 1 to 6), the deposit you pick with its commodity, rigs, amount, density and notes to set, the deposits round you in the order to drive them, and figures for rigs down, ground swept, the rig plan, the Rhino's hold and an estimated value. SITES has your saved mining locations, your worked-out deposits and every deposit you've marked anywhere, each one editable from there whether you're in the Rhino or not; WHERE TO LAND ranks this system's Rhino ground. The Mining page keeps the run and the holds and points across.
* **A map that holds still.** The Rhino page's map has its own view, apart from the Rhino Map overlay's range: it opens fitted to the mining location you're working (its deposits, rigs, rig plan, survey area and you) and stays put while you drive about inside it. Scroll or + and − to zoom (down to 100 m round) and it stays there; drag to pan; RECENTRE or a double-click fits it again. Click a deposit to pick it.
* **The page follows you.** Its map and keys come up when you're in the Rhino; arriving at a planet in the ship it waits for you to deploy. Back aboard after a run it shows the run instead: refined, rigs still out, both holds, how long, the site and what's next, with the rig buttons to put a rig right and the map without the rig radar. A restart in the ship keeps it.
* **Go there, and stop.** GO THERE on a deposit or TARGET on a saved site points the planet compass at it. The target shows over the map and in SITES with how far and which way, CLEAR TARGET drops it, and it clears by itself as you arrive (40 m from a deposit, 200 m from a site's centre).
* **Each site's runs.** Dock the Rhino after working a saved site and the run is kept with it: how long, what it refined and by commodity, and its tonnes an hour. SITES shows each site's runs, tonnes and usual rate (open RUNS for the list, best picked out), so you can see which places are worth going back to; BACK ABOARD says which run here this was and the best before it.
* **One mining location at a time.** Everything the Rhino tools show is the location you're at, within 8 km of you: the survey, planning circle, marked deposits, refining spots, best patch and values (before, a survey and best patch stayed with the whole planet). Drive or fly more than 8 km away, or reach another signal 2 km or more off, and the location you left is saved to SITES and its survey and circle put away, so the next one starts clean. Deposits elsewhere on the body are kept: the page counts them, and they're back when you are.
* **Save the site you've surveyed.** SAVE THIS SITE on the Rhino page (name it there if you like, or use its key in Settings › Hotkeys › Rhino) keeps the mining location: its name and signal number, its centre, the survey with what you've swept, the deposits inside it, your rig plans and the tonnes your rigs took there. You don't have to press it: a location with a survey or marked deposits is kept as you work it, and once more as you leave; the same site again (its signal at the same place, or within 2 km) is brought up to date, not saved twice. Saved sites stay until you delete them: rename them, add a note, TARGET one's centre, RESUME its survey when you're back on the planet, or DELETE it (it asks again; its deposits stay).
* **The survey, done right.** Marking the centre used to paint a guessed 3 km area with the scanner's 2 km reach straight away, turning the Rhino Map into one tinted plate. Now only the centre shows (with how far you are from it) until you mark the edge; your drive is kept meanwhile and swept in when you do, so nothing covered on the way out is lost. An edge pressed within 500 m of the centre is taken for the key pressed twice. Coverage is kept in 50 m squares, the way between two position updates counts as swept, and the figure reads 100% only when all of it is.
* **Mark the deposits.** Drive onto a deposit and press the new key (Ctrl+Alt+9): it goes on the map with what's being refined round it and the mining location's number. A rig dropped on it takes its commodity; one marked before anything's been refined there is named by the first rig to work it. Set its rigs, amount and density on the Rhino page; a second key marks it mined out.
* **Worked-out deposits, and when to look again.** Deposits grow back, and nobody knows quite how fast, so the app keeps an honest record instead of guessing: when each was first worked out, and each time you went back and found it still empty. Two weeks on it's due a look: a yellow RECHECK ring on the maps, and you're told when you drive up to it. MINED OUT on it (or STILL DEPLETED on the Rhino page) if it's still empty, and the mark comes off a week later; a rig refining its commodity there again marks it grown back by itself (REGROWN does it by hand). SITES lists them all, due a look first, with TARGET to go there.
* **The Rhino's commodities first.** The commodity box offers the thirteen surface commodities (Bastnäsite to Thortveitite) first, then every other one you've found with the Rhino, then the rest; SITES has them as quick filters. Settings › Flight & exploration › Rhino commodities switches off the ones you never mine.
* **When a rig is ready, learnt from yours.** How long a rig takes isn't published and the journal doesn't say, so the app times your own: each collected rig, from its key down to its key up, by the density of the deposit it was on. Once a few are timed, each dial fills a ring to READY (on the Rhino page too: how long still to go), and your companion tells you; until then the dial counts up how long it's been down rather than guessing.
* **The crew answers every rig.** Placing, collecting, a rig ready, the last one in, one about to be lost: your companion says each, whatever its chat setting (unless it's off), instead of the odd one when it felt like talking.
* **Rigs where they land, and what they gave.** A rig is marked where it drops, 7 m behind the cockpit (Overlay Studio › Rig Tracker sets it, 0 to 20 m). Each tonne refined counts for the rig that threw it up, so every rig shows what it's working and how many tonnes it has given, on the map, the Rig Tracker, the HUD and the Local API. The rig keys only work from the SRV, so one pressed in the ship can't collect a rig by mistake (the Rhino page's buttons put things right from anywhere).
* **The rig planner.** Press Ctrl+Alt+0 and drive round a deposit's edge back to where you started: up to six rig spots (P1 to P6) fit inside it at your rig spacing, in the order to drive them, and each ticks off as a rig goes down. How many fit (seven or more is possible) goes on the deposit as its RIGS IT TAKES if you haven't set it, and a deposit marked on traced ground takes it too, so its value shows sooner. Every Rhino view says RIG OK HERE, RIG OK HERE · JUST (amber, within 2 m of a ring), or how far and which way the nearest spot a rig can go is.
* **The planning circle.** A key puts a 4.5 km circle round where you stand: rig and drive inside it and no rig gets left the 5 km behind that loses it. Its room left shows on the map, and you're told once if you drive out of it.
* **Values from your own rigs.** A deposit's worth is its rigs times what your collected rigs actually gave (once you've worked three) times what you've been paid for that commodity, or its usual price. Before that, no value is shown rather than a guess.
* **Maps that measure the ground properly.** Distances and directions on the Rhino Map, the Planetary map and the survey are worked out across the planet's surface, so they stay true on small moons and near the poles, where the old flat map stretched.
* **The Rhino Map, reworked.** Deposits in their commodity's colour, numbered in driving order with their range and rigs; the best patches (five rigs' worth or more together) with what they come to an hour; lines to the nearest deposit of what you're working and of the next cheaper commodity; the traced edge and its pins; the planning circle; a header with the deposits, rigs and value here; and names that never overlap ("+3 MORE" when there's no room). NEXT in the strip has a turn arrow and the patch's worth.
* **The Rig Tracker, the Rhino's all-in-one.** Under the rig dials (each with what it's working and the tonnes it has given, and a ring that fills to READY): the guide (the step you're on, its key and what comes next, moving on by itself), the mineral deposit you're nearest, the nearest marked deposits with what each commodity here is worth, and the status (value, drive, what a rig gives, the best patches), with a red bar for high gravity or a rig about to be lost. Each part switches off in Overlay Studio › Rig Tracker. Back in the ship after a run it becomes BACK ABOARD rather than vanishing: the run's length, what was refined and by commodity, any rig still out and how far, the site and what's next, until you leave the planet or take the Rhino out again.
* **The Dashboard, in two lanes.** No more cards with gaps between them: the wide lane on the left holds this system (its worlds and priority list are back), the route and the live feed; the narrow lane on the right stacks what's under way (crew, orders, mining, trade…), the session, your priorities and the Codex hunt. Each lane is a plain stack, so panels that come and go never leave a hole. Deck Options now sets each panel's lane and order and shows or hides it; Arrange panels no longer applies to the Dashboard. The fuel, data-aboard and source tiles are gone (the top bar and status bar say them), and open survey work moved into the system card.
* **The Focused Log, rebuilt as a log.** Made for its small window and fits it without scrolling: where you are along the top, what to do next with your companion's latest word (by name, not always "the Watcher"), a strip of what's under way (the survey, route, data aboard and session while exploring; the rock, the trade or the station when that's what you're doing), up to three things still to do, and the feed filling the rest. **⧉ POP OUT** (in the log, or ⧉ LOG beside Focused Log on the Dashboard) opens it in a window of its own and puts the deck back, so you can keep using the rest of the app.
* **Overlay groups.** Make groups of overlays for whatever you're doing in Overlay Studio's new GROUPS card: a name, its overlays, and its type. "Only this group" hides every other overlay while it's on; "Add these" shows its overlays while on and hides them while off, leaving the rest alone. Tie a group to an activity (exploring, ring mining, in the Rhino, on a planet, on foot, docked, in combat or jumping) and it comes on by itself while you're doing it and goes when you stop; switch one by hand and it stays as you left it. Up to eight, each with its own hotkey (Settings › Hotkeys › Controls: Group 1 to 8, by its place in the list), and they lead the overlay switches in the deck header. An overlay in a group that's off is turned on when the group comes on.
* **Skins: themes with their own fonts and shapes.** Eleven new themes go further than colour: Cockpit HUD, Starliner, Station Blue, Imperial Gold, Crystal, Ember, Umbral, Obsidian Red, Verdigris, Ochre Squadron and Midnight each bring their own lettering, corners and panel styling to the deck and the overlays. Pick one in Settings › Appearance like any other theme (its card says SKIN); it applies at once, overlays included.
* **Crew chatter flows.** When your companions talk among themselves, each reply now follows the line before it as soon as that's been said and read, instead of waiting for the next idle thought, up to half a minute later.
* **The pad card goes when you leave.** The Dashboard's landing pad diagram cleared only after a quarter of an hour, so it came back once you'd undocked. It now goes as you undock, cancel or are denied docking, or jump out.
* **A new key never takes one you've set.** When a new action's default shortcut is one you'd already given another action, the default steps aside (both would have stopped working, and Settings › Hotkeys refused every save until one was cleared). So if you'd put a Rhino survey key on Ctrl+Alt+9, the new Mark deposit key starts unset instead.
* **Target sounds removed.** The fanfare, upbeat pair and thud as you target a system are gone, and so is their switch in Settings › Crew calls. What your companion says (or the notification) on targeting stays. (if you turned them off you can now turn it back on, it won't play those odd sounds anymore)
* **Rhino mining in the Local API.** GET /api/v1/rhino for the survey, plan, deposits (with where a worked-out one is in its timeline), patches, value and the saved site you're at; GET /api/v1/rigs adds the back-aboard summary; POST /api/v1/rhino/deposit, /mined-out, /trace, /circle and /save do what their keys do.
