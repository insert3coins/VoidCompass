# VoidCompass // UPDATE LOG

## v5.8.4 // Nuts and Bolts
**Release Date:** TBA 3312 *(Galactic Standard Time)*

* **Materials: engineering, on foot and in your ships, on a page of its own.** A new page under FIELD SYSTEMS that works out what to do next, not just what you have. It's all read from your journal: no screen reading, nothing to run beside the game. ON FOOT and SHIP each have five tabs, PLAN, HOLD, RECIPES, BUILD and SOURCES, and OTHER has community goals and permits.
  * **PLAN:** the wishlist at a glance (how much of it you hold, how many goals are ready to make, how many materials are short, the fees), then one view at a time. NEXT STEPS is worked out from everything the page knows: which rolls are ready to make and the engineer to take them to (the one you pinned, else unlocked and nearest), material trader swaps from what the list doesn't need (with the nearest trader once looked up), what to collect and where (High Grade Emissions in the states that gave you that material, wakes, surface prospecting), the bartender, Pioneer Supplies for suit and weapon grades, and on foot the few kinds of settlement building that turn up most of what you're short of; a step goes as soon as the journal sees it done. GOALS lists them ready to make first, then in progress closest to done, then any left out, with what's next for each ("G2: 1 of 2 rolled"), and your pinned blueprints. SHOPPING LIST is every material the list needs against what you hold, short ones or everything, by kind or by where to get it (the trader swap that covers it, High Grade Emissions, scanning, the settlement buildings), with how many goals use each. ENGINEERS is every engineer the list needs, ready or not, in the fewest stops. As many wishlists as you like for each side: switch, rename, share as a code or IMPORT one.
  * **One inspector instead of buttons everywhere.** Rows and cards stay quiet; pick anything (a goal, a step, an item, a material, a blueprint, an engineer) and the inspector beside the list shows all of it with what you can do: a goal's materials and its grades and how many, LEAVE IT OUT, MADE IT; a material's cap, how to get it, what the trader would swap for it and what uses it; a blueprint's every grade with who makes it and what it takes, + WISHLIST and PIN TO an engineer; an engineer's place, how to meet and unlock them, what they make and what you've pinned to them.
  * **Find anything:** one search at the top of the page for a material, on-foot item, blueprint or engineer, straight into the inspector.
  * **HOLD:** on foot, every good, asset, data and consumable in your ship locker and backpack with what it's worth and what it's for, filtered by your wishlist, what's used, what isn't (safe to sell) or your backpack; mark any item USED or NOT USED to overrule the recipes. For your ship, raw, manufactured and encoded laid out like the material trader, family by family and grade by grade against their caps (Guardian and Thargoid ones too, the Titan war's included), and your cargo and carrier trade orders.
  * **RECIPES,** by item or by engineer, as grade grids you can read at a glance: each blueprint (or suit and weapon) a row, each grade a column, marked ✓ when you can make it now (✓3: three times), ◐ when some of it is in hand, ✗ when none is. Ship modules sit in a picker grouped CORE, WEAPONS, UTILITY and OPTIONAL, with a mark on the ones your wishlist uses; pick a cell for that grade's materials and who makes it, or a name for every grade, + WISHLIST and PIN. Every ship blueprint in the game data, the newest included: the mining lasers and Abrasion Blaster, seeker, enzyme and AX missile racks, mine launchers, module reinforcement, Prismatic shields and caustic sink launchers have theirs, and the latest blueprints (Scoop Rate Enhanced, Plasma Conversion, Rapid Phase and the rest) are in, the Merc blueprints with the Merc Coins each roll costs. Experimentals have the same module picker (the ones that suit any weapon listed with every weapon), technology broker unlocks a picker by broker and what they unlock (the commodities they ask for counted from your hold), and synthesis a picker by ship, ammunition and SRV, each then a short list (its levels, for synthesis) with how much of it you hold. On foot, a suit or weapon opens with its grade upgrades and every mod against what you hold. Every engineer, nearest first, with how far you've got with each. PIN any ship blueprint to the engineer you'll take it to, at their best grade; SHARE PINS copies them as a code.
  * **BUILD:** on foot, design a loadout (a suit and up to three weapons, the grade each is at and is going to, the mods fitted and wanted) and see the shields, battery, capacity, damage, magazine and range change; copy one of your in-game loadouts to start from, and + WISHLIST adds what it takes. For your ship, the ship builder: any of 49 hulls (the Anaconda Mk II and Lynx Highliner included, and every current module, Mk II passenger cabins, vessel and vehicle hangars and cargo racks among them; newer blueprints fit as MATERIALS ONLY until you engineer one: each roll you make, and every engineered module in your journal history, teaches the builder what that grade does, marked LEARNED FIGURES; and a new ship the tables don't have yet, once you've flown it, is a hull of its own under NEW HULL, its slots, modules and mass learned from your journal), or the ship you're flying with the game's own engineered figures, fit and engineer each slot (blueprint, grade, rolls per slot, experimental) and read the mass, jump range, speed and boost, shields, armour, resistances, power, heat, damage and cost; save, CLONE, COMPARE with another build, share as a short code (around 200 characters for a typical ship), each saved build a file of its own named after it in your profile's ship_builds folder (put a build file in there and it loads next time), IMPORT BUILD (SLEF, a journal Loadout or an EDMC ship link), and + WISHLIST puts its engineering on your ship wishlist.
  * **SOURCES:** on foot, the bartender (what your spare items would fetch against what's still short) and the settlement buildings where your short items turn up. For your ship, the nearest material traders and technology brokers (Spansh), the swaps for your wishlist, and every High Grade Emission you've worked: the system, the faction and its states, what you picked up, what each state has given you, and the materials only Empire or Federation systems give.
  * **Your engineering moves the wishlists on.** Each blueprint roll you make at an engineer comes off the matching ship wishlist item, a grade finished moves it on, and it leaves the list at its grade; applying an experimental takes that off too. Suit and weapon grade upgrades move on the same way, each counted once however often the journal is read back. MADE IT is there for on-foot mods, which the journal doesn't report.
  * **Imports:** IMPORT on PLAN's wishlist bar takes a shared wishlist or pins code, an edomh:// link, an EDSY or Coriolis wishlist (best-grade percentage and experimentals included), or a build's SLEF to turn its engineering into a wishlist. ON FOOT › BUILD imports a shared loadout design, and the ship builder's IMPORT BUILD takes SLEF, a journal Loadout, an EDMC ship link or a shared build's code (it opens straight away).
  * **Bringing a build over from EDSY:** open the build in EDSY, go to its Outfitting page and press **Export...**, then copy the text in the **SLEF** box and paste it into IMPORT BUILD in the ship builder (or into IMPORT on PLAN's wishlist bar for a wishlist of its engineering). A link to the build itself (edsy.org/#/L=...) is EDSY's own code, which only EDSY can read; the page says so and how to export instead. Coriolis works the same way: export the build as SLEF. A ship the builder's tables don't have yet comes in with all its slots and modules, and modules and blueprints it doesn't know yet are kept by name.
  * **COMMUNITY GOALS:** each goal you've seen with its total, your contribution, your band, your reward, its tier and a chart of how it's moved. **PERMITS:** every permit-locked system and how its permit is earned.
  * **Settings › Flight & exploration › MATERIALS:** a note each time you pick up something a wishlist is short of (on foot or in your ship), the ready-to-make note, hiding unused items in the locker, the locker's order, and the ship builder's roll quality.
  * Other apps can read it too: GET /api/v1/materials gives what you hold, engineer progress, each side's wishlist worked out, the engineers to visit, and your High Grade Emissions.

* **Hide on maps and Hide in menus, overlay by overlay.** There's no longer a switch for all overlays sitting above each overlay's own: each overlay's Hide on maps and Hide in menus in Overlay Studio's inspector is the whole rule, and it works the moment you change it. The VISIBILITY card at the top keeps Hide when Elite is not running and Layout mode, and for the maps and the menus has HIDE ALL and SHOW ALL to set every overlay at once, with how many hide in each. Your old choices carry over: if map hiding was off for all, every overlay now stays on the maps; if menu hiding was on, every overlay you hadn't kept on screen now hides in the menus.

* **A smaller Captain's Log file.** Sessions older than your latest 30 keep their moments (codex entries, biology, sales, photos, losses, milestones) and their counts, without a line for every jump and survey, so captains_log.json stays around a third of the size it was.
