# VoidCompass // UPDATE LOG

## v5.7.1 // What Should I Call You?
**Release Date:** 1 NOV 3312 *(Galactic Standard Time)*

* **The Watcher learns your name.** The ship writes your name first thing every time you wake it, and the Watcher has read it every session without saying a word. Once you two have been together a while, a new thread of its story begins: The Name. It notices the name, tries it out, and asks whether it may use it (your name, Commander, or a name you choose). Say yes, and you'll hear it for the first time. Commander stays its everyday word; your name it keeps for the moments that matter, never more than once a session: coming home, after a loss, a first discovery, a promotion, a goodnight. Change your name and it notices.
* **Your name, said properly.** A new YOUR NAME card on the Watcher page's YOU tab: what it calls you, a name you choose, and how it says your name aloud (it works out a tag like insert3coins by itself; write it the way it sounds if it doesn't), with HEAR IT.
* **What your journal says of you.** Promotions by their real names ("Elite, in exploration"), and now and then a word about your record (the systems you've visited, your jumps, your hours), the power you pledged to, your squadron and your carrier.
* **A better speaking voice.** Its little sounds are said, not spelled: Hm is a thoughtful hum (it used to be "aitch em"), Mm, Mm-hm, Hmph, Shh and Pfft too. Ship marks are said as words (Cobra Mk III is "Mark three", Elite III is "Elite three"), kilometres and AU in full, SCO and EDSM as letters, and a word in capitals for emphasis is said as a word, not spelled out. Each fix was checked against the sounds the voice engine actually makes.
* **The Watcher's first word after the boot screen.** A thought that showed while the boot screen held the voice is now said when it lifts, even with long thought holds.
* **Overlays come and go together.** Back from the Galaxy Map, after the boot screen, or whenever several overlays show or hide at once, they now appear and vanish in the same frame instead of popping in one after another. They load and draw out of sight behind the boot screen, and a map visit keeps them drawn the same way, so they're there the instant it lifts or closes.
* **Its own words (experimental).** The Watcher can now run a small language model on your own PC (Qwen3.5, via llama.cpp; nothing leaves your computer). Switch it on in the new MIND tab on the Watcher page. Awake, it says everything in its own words and its own nature; asleep or still waking, it uses its written lines as always.
  * **Talk to it.** Type in TALK TO IT and it answers beside the orb, aloud with the voice on. Ask how far the route goes, how the ship is doing, what's left to scan, what your colony still needs, how you stand in Powerplay, how the expedition is going or what Galnet says, and it answers from what Void Compass shows right now. Ask what could live on a world (the one you name, the one you're at, or this system's worlds with signals) and it answers from your scans and Survey's own predictions: the genera, what they'd fetch, and their colours.
  * **Talk to it by voice.** Hold your talk key (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F8 by default, in Settings › Hotkeys) or the microphone button by SAY IT, speak, and let go: it answers as if you'd typed it. Prefer to tap once to start and again to stop? That's a setting. A ring on the orb shows it's listening, so you know in game. Choose the microphone, test it (with a level meter, what it heard and the sound to hear back) under TALK BY VOICE on the MIND tab. It hears you with Whisper (small, English) on this PC only: switching it on downloads 497 MB once, the microphone is open only while you talk, and nothing it hears is kept.
  * **Small things, when asked.** Pause, play or skip the music, hide or show the overlays, copy the next system, or keep quiet for a while (and speak again).
  * **It learns from your flying.** Every jump, dock, discovery, sale, bio logged and ship swap goes into its memory straight from your journal (WHAT IT HAS SEEN LATELY). It keeps a diary of each session you flew together (WHAT IT WROTE DOWN), has the odd thought of its own, and can tell you what you did last time. Its mood and its trust in you colour how it speaks.
  * **Its LIBRARY.** A new tab with everything it may draw on, with search first: 119 things it knows of the galaxy (places, powers, ships, flying, exploration, exobiology, mining and trade, engineering, life on foot, carriers and colonies), the notes it took from your talks (each yours to delete), a few sentences about you that it condenses now and then, TEACH IT for anything you want it to know, and how much of its story it may speak of. It searches by meaning: a small extra model on the CPU reads what each card means, so "What happens if I can't pay the upkeep?" finds the page about indebted carriers, not just the same words. Without it, it matches words.
  * **Your sources.** Give it pages about Elite Dangerous to read, such as a wiki article or a guide, and optionally the pages they link to, up to a limit you choose. It reads them on this PC and adds what they say to its LIBRARY, searchable there. When you talk to it, it reads the best few cards for what you asked ("How many landing pads does a carrier have?" comes back with 16: eight large, four medium, four small).
    * Wikis are read through their own interface for programs.
    * Each site's robots.txt is obeyed, and pages are read one at a time.
    * Nothing that names what the Watcher never speaks of is kept.
    * Read any source again, or remove it with everything it gave.
  * **Its voice, yours to shape.** 👍 or 👎 any line it said in its own words, and its talk replies (ITS VOICE on the MIND tab). A liked line becomes its tone for that moment; for a disliked one, it steers clear of anything like it. Take a vote back any time.
  * **Quicker, on the CPU most of all.** It keeps what doesn't change between requests (who it is, its rules, what it knows of you) and reads only what's new. A mix of thoughts and talk took 20 seconds on a CPU, down from 47.
  * **Pages in its own words.** Its note on each new postcard, the newest nights' flight logs on the Captain's Log and Void Wrapped's verdict, and at sign-off a goodnight that recaps the session. The written ones show until its words are back.
  * **It keeps to its rules.** Its story and its questions stay exactly as written, and it speaks only of the parts of its story it has already told you. It knows only what your journal, Void Compass and its memory say, plus the galaxy's basics. Words that break its rules are never shown: a made-up number or place, a name it mustn't say, another language, talk of AI or of its own rules, a line copied from another. Replies that run long are cut back to whole sentences. Ask about the mysteries it never speaks of, and it answers in its own way.
  * **Setting it up.** Off by default. Switching it on downloads llama.cpp (32 MB), its library search model (37 MB; already woken, it fetches just that) and the model you choose: Standard (2.7 GB, recommended) or Light (1.3 GB, lighter on memory, a little less sure of its words). It runs on the graphics card (AMD, NVIDIA or Intel) or the CPU, below the game's priority, and can free its memory when idle. On the CPU it takes a few seconds a line, and it waits longer for its words there. Ask it something while it's waking (just switched on, or another model) and it answers as soon as it's awake.
* **Survey after a jump.** Jumping into a known system straight from a busier one could leave an empty gap in the Survey overlay, or cut off its lower lists at the bottom of the window. It now fits the new system exactly.
* **Hide overlays in menus.** A new Overlay Studio switch hides them on the ship panels, in station services, the Codex, carrier management, Powerplay, Squadrons and the main menu, with a choice per overlay, just like the maps. Construction Needs stays by default. The game says nothing when the pause menu opens, so that one can't be covered.
